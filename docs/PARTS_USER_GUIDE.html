Where is “Issue”?

The most common question, answered first

Short answer

The part is still in quarantine. Newly received stock lands in quarantine and cannot be issued to anything. The Move this stock panel — the one holding Issue to a job — only appears once the unit is available.

Open the unit, complete the Receiving inspection panel, and the movement controls appear.

This is deliberate. The workspace ships a quarantine-then-inspect policy: nothing reaches the serviceable shelf without somebody recording what they checked. A part that has not been inspected has no business going onto an aircraft, so the system does not offer the option.

Two other reasons the panel can be missing:

  • The unit is issued, shipped, scrapped or archived. These are terminal — the drawer says so and offers nothing but the record. An issued part can still be returned to stock if it came back unused.
  • Your role cannot write to parts at all. A Viewer, Planner, or Controller sees the records and no controls.

A unit’s life

Every “can I do this?” follows from where the unit is

quarantine → available → reserved → issued the ordinary path
quarantine → rejected or hold_ncm inspection failed
hold_ncm → available discrepancy resolved “accept as is”
available → shipped / scrapped / in_repair out of stock, other ways
issued → available came back unused

Records are archived, never deleted. Every move writes a line to the stock ledger with who did it, when, and what it referenced — that ledger is the record, not the current status.

If a control is missing, this table says why

Receiving a part

From the box to the shelf

  1. Start a receiving draft

    From Inventory, begin a receipt. Photograph the tag and the data plate — the system reads them and proposes values.

  2. Review what it read

    Extraction output is a suggestion. Accept, edit, or reject each field. Nothing it proposes is treated as confirmed, and it never certifies identity, condition, or airworthiness.

  3. Confirm the receipt

    The unit and its first ledger entry are written together. It lands in quarantine.

  4. Inspect it

    Open the unit. The Receiving inspection panel asks five questions, each pass, fail, or n/a:

    • Part number matches the order
    • Serial number matches the tag
    • Tag present and legible
    • Shelf life acceptable
    • Dangerous goods paperwork

    n/a is a real answer, not a blank. Missing dangerous-goods paperwork is a pass for a part that is not dangerous goods and a fail for one that is.

    Record the tag you actually read and the condition you actually found. Leave Outcome on Follow the gates and the system decides; the only manual choice is holding a part whose gates all passed, which your judgement is allowed to override.

  5. It is now on the shelf

    A clean inspection releases the unit to available and stamps it with the tag you recorded. The inspection itself is kept as the evidence behind that release.

A part cannot be accepted over a failed gate

If any gate is fail, or you ticked shipping damage, the unit stays in quarantine no matter what you choose. The system will name the gate that stopped it.

Issuing a part

Getting stock onto a job

Open the unit from Inventory. In Move this stock:

  1. Enter the job reference

    The Job, case, or order reference field is required. Issuing without one is refused — stock that left the shelf for nothing traceable is stock nobody can account for.

  2. Press “Issue to a job”

    The unit becomes issued and the ledger records the reference, you, and the time.

Reserve first, issue later

If the part is spoken for but not yet being fitted, Reserve it against the same reference. Reserved stock stops counting as free supply on the Shortages tab, so nobody orders against it twice. Issue it when it actually goes out, or Release reservation if the job changes.

If it comes back

An issued unit that was never fitted gets Return to stock. Name the bin it goes back into. It returns to available with the round trip on its ledger.

When something is wrong

Holding material and deciding what happens to it

Anything wrong with received material — wrong part, short count, damage, missing or illegible paperwork, expired shelf life, a condition that does not match what was ordered, or a suspected unapproved part — goes on a discrepancy.

Raising one from the unit drawer does two things: it records what is wrong, and it moves the unit to hold_ncm so it cannot be issued while the question is open.

Suspected unapproved parts

Choosing Suspected unapproved part also flags the unit itself, with your summary as the stated reason. That flag travels with the part rather than living only in the report, and it cannot be set without saying why.

Closing it out

Go to the Discrepancies tab and pick a disposition:

DispositionWhat happens to the part
accept as isReleased to available, and any suspected-unapproved flag is cleared. Needs a quality, manager, or administrator role.
return to vendorStays held. Ship it out as a separate, recorded movement.
reworkStays held. Move it to repair yourself when it goes.
scrapStays held. Scrap it as its own movement, which is terminal.

Only accept as is releases the part, and only when nothing else is still open against it. The other three leave the material held on purpose: the decision and the movement are separate acts, and the movement is yours to record.

Counts, splits, returns

Lots, and getting the number right

Counting a lot

Enter what you actually found on the shelf. The ledger records the signed difference from what the system believed it held — not a silent overwrite. If you counted 47 against a believed 50, the ledger says −3.

Splitting a lot

Break a quantity off a lot into its own unit when part of it is going somewhere separately. Both the remainder and the new unit carry a split event naming the other, so the ledger explains where the stock came from and where it went.

Correcting details

Correct details changes serial number, lot number, condition, and tag, with the previous values recorded. Quantity, status, and location are deliberately not editable here — those move through their own actions so every change to them lands on the ledger.

Who can do what

Releasing stock to the shelf is a buy-off, and is held tighter

  • ViewerRead everything. No controls.
  • PlannerRead everything. No controls.
  • ControllerRead everything. No controls.
  • TechnicianReceive, move, issue, count, split, correct, raise discrepancies. Cannot accept stock into serviceable inventory.
  • ProcurementSame as Technician.
  • QualityAll of the above, plus accepting stock into serviceable inventory and accepting non-conforming material as is.
  • ManagerAs Quality.
  • AdministratorAs Quality.

Rejecting or holding a part moves in the conservative direction, so it stays open to anyone who may receive one. Only releasing to the shelf is restricted.

Why it asks twice

Confirmation on anything that moves stock

Every action that changes the ledger — confirming a receipt, inspecting, moving, correcting, adjusting a count, splitting, raising or resolving a discrepancy — is confirmed by you at the moment you do it. That confirmation is single-use and tied to that exact unit at that exact version.

Two consequences worth knowing:

  • If somebody else changed the unit while you had it open, your action is refused rather than silently overwriting theirs. Reopen the unit and redo it against what is now there.
  • A confirmation cannot be reused. Each action confirms itself; there is no way to approve once and act repeatedly.

When it refuses

What the message means and what to do

What you seeWhat it meansWhat to do
“requires referenceId identifying the job or order it serves” You issued, reserved, or shipped without a reference. Fill in the job, case, or order field.
“a part cannot be accepted with …” You chose to accept over a failed gate or shipping damage. Fix the gate if you mis-clicked, or let the outcome follow the gates and raise a discrepancy.
“expected version N, current version is M” Somebody changed the unit while you had it open. Reopen the unit and redo the action against what is there now.
“a unit in available is not awaiting inspection” You tried to inspect something already released. Nothing to do. To pull it back, raise a discrepancy.
“only a quality, manager, or administrator role can …” Releasing to the serviceable shelf is a buy-off. Ask an inspector to complete it.
“a unit in X cannot move to Y” That is not a legal transition. Check the lifecycle above for the route.
“the destination is the location the unit already occupies” You transferred a unit to its own bin. Name a different destination.
A signed confirmation is required The action reached the server without its confirmation. Retry from the drawer. If it persists, the environment is missing its confirmation configuration — tell whoever runs it.
